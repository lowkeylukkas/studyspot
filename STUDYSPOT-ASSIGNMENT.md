# StudySpot — Design Sprint Deliverables

## Selected concept

**Option A — Study Spot Finder**

**Website name:** StudySpot  
**Tagline:** Find your space. Find your focus.  
**Primary call to action:** Find my study spot

### One-sentence value proposition

StudySpot helps students quickly discover an available campus study space that matches their preferred atmosphere and reserve a seat without wandering from building to building.

## Target persona

**Alex — Grade 11 student**

- **Needs:** A convenient place to study between classes, reliable seating, and access to power outlets.
- **Pain points:** Arriving at a crowded study area, walking around campus to find an open seat, and not knowing whether a space is quiet or group-friendly.
- **Goals:** Find a suitable place quickly, make productive use of breaks, and avoid wasting time searching for a seat.
- **How StudySpot helps:** A searchable directory with filters and a simple reservation flow makes space discovery more predictable.

## Core features

1. **Searchable campus directory** — Search by space name, building, or keyword.
2. **Useful filters** — Narrow results by Quiet, Moderate, or Group atmosphere, available seats, and power outlets.
3. **Simple reservation flow** — Select a space, enter student details, choose a session duration, and view a confirmation screen.

## Wireframe descriptions

### Wireframe 1 — Homepage / landing page

- Header: StudySpot logo, Find a spot, How it works, About.
- Hero headline: “Find your space. Find your focus.”
- Supporting copy explains the value of finding a campus seat before walking to the location.
- Primary CTA: “Find my study spot” links to the directory.
- Supporting proof/highlight strip and a three-step overview.
- Footer with StudySpot branding.

### Wireframe 2 — Study-space directory

- Section heading: “Your spot is out there.”
- Search field for name, building, or keyword.
- Filters for study vibe, seats open, and power outlets.
- Result cards show image, name, building, floor, study atmosphere, remaining seats, hours, and amenities.
- Each card has a Reserve action.

### Wireframe 3 — Reservation flow

- Modal opens from a selected study-space card.
- Selected space summary shows name, building, floor, and sample seat availability.
- Form collects name, student ID, and session duration.
- Submit action leads to a confirmation state that summarizes the reservation.
- The prototype clearly states that it is a demo and does not create a real campus booking.

## Two-minute pitch script

### 0:00–0:30 — The problem and persona

“Have you ever had a free period, wanted to study, and spent more time looking for a seat than actually studying? That is the problem StudySpot aims to solve. Our target student is Alex, a Grade 11 learner who needs a convenient place to focus between classes. Alex does not always know which campus spaces are crowded, quiet, or equipped with power outlets.”

### 0:30–1:30 — Product walkthrough

“StudySpot gives students one place to explore campus study spaces. From the homepage, the Find my study spot button takes students straight to the directory. They can search for a building or a specific space, then filter by Quiet, Moderate, or Group study vibes. They can also narrow the results to spaces with available seats or power outlets. Each card shows the location, floor, atmosphere, seat count, operating hours, and amenities. When Alex finds a suitable space, the Reserve button opens a short form. Alex enters a name and student ID, chooses a session duration, and receives a confirmation summary.”

### 1:30–2:00 — CTA and value

“Our main call to action is Find my study spot because it takes students directly to the most important task: discovering a space that works for them. The flow is simple: explore, find your vibe, and save your seat. StudySpot reduces the guesswork around campus study spaces so students can spend less time searching and more time learning.”

## Prototype interaction notes

- Search and filters update the visible space cards immediately.
- Reserve opens a reservation form for the selected space.
- Submitting the form displays a confirmation summary and decreases the sample seat count by one.
- All availability values and reservations are demo-only and reset when the app is refreshed.

## Future improvements

- Real-time room occupancy and seat availability.
- Student account authentication and verified student IDs.
- Campus map and directions to each space.
- Server-side bookings, cancellation, and reservation time limits.
- Accessibility information and study-space equipment details.

## Design annotations

- **Primary CTA:** “Find my study spot” — directs visitors to the directory.
- **Search:** Filters spaces by name, building, description, and tags.
- **Study vibe:** Helps students match a space to their preferred working environment.
- **Seats open:** Shows only spaces with at least one sample seat available.
- **Power outlets:** Helps students find spaces suitable for charging devices.
- **Reserve:** Opens the selected space's booking form.
- **Confirmation:** Confirms the details entered in the demo reservation flow.
- **Prototype notice:** Makes it clear that sample data is not live campus availability.
